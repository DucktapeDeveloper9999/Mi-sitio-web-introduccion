Welcome to my website!


Welcome to my introductory website, where I will introduce myself and share a bit about who I am. On this site, I will talk about myself and my journey as a programmer (though I am still a beginner in this field). I will also share the story of how I started programming, as well as my ideas, goals, and objectives, both those I have now and those I hope to achieve in the future. P.S.: Please keep in mind that this website will be updated frequently in the future. Thank you for your attention and happy reading!


Who I am?


Hello, my name is Valerio. I am 21 years old and a programming student. I live in the Netherlands, more specifically in Deventer, a city located in the province of Overijssel. I currently live with my family: my mother, my younger sister, and my cat. I am currently studying Software Developer course at Deltion College, located in Zwolle.

I really enjoy school because there is so much to learn and many interesting people to meet. Furthermore, if I find something difficult, I can ask my teachers or classmates for help or advice. Some of my classmates have a great chunk of experience in programming, and I love learning from them.

Outside of school, I really enjoy traveling, exploring new cities and countries, and discovering different cultures and traditions. I am also passionate about cooking, art, anime, movies, and TV series. Additionally, I have an interest in science (especially paleontology, genetics, and evolution), history, technology, and programming. To be honest, I realize it is challenging to be knowledgeable across so many fields, but that is one of the traits that makes me stand out. While it is true that I am not an expert in every subject that interests me, I am able to quickly learn and grasp most of the concepts and ideas that capture my interest.

However, I sometimes find it quite difficult to apply my knowledge to real-life situations, and that is something I want to improve. Even so, when I feel frustrated or overwhelmed by life, I try to find new solutions or different ways to resolve my problems. I always strive to keep moving forward, no matter how high an obstacle might be, how complicated it is, or how much time it takes to overcome. These are some of the things that define me as a person. I am quite proud of who I am, though I also know that I still have a great deal to learn and improve upon—both in programming and in life. I have a lot of work ahead of me, and I am putting in all my effort to grow as both a programmer and an individual. I am also quite impatient; sometimes that impatience leads me to make mistakes."



My first steps as an programmer


In this section, I want to share my first steps into the world of programming and how the idea of becoming a programmer came to me. My introduction to programming was quite unexpected. It all began when I was an intern at OOZ Zwolle, an organization dedicated to improving the quality of education for children and young people up to the age of 18. I worked there as an intern in the IT department, though most of my tasks had nothing to do with programming.

I realize it’s rather unusual for an intern to take on tasks unrelated to their assigned role, but there is an explanation. You see, I wasn’t the only intern; a classmate of mine from school was also working there. We got along very well, and both of us were highly motivated to work, learn new things, and put our knowledge into practice. However, after the first month, it became clear that there wasn’t enough work for both of us.

At first, we did everything we were asked to do, but there still wasn’t much to keep us busy. When there was nothing to do, I would play games on my laptop—something my supervisors weren’t particularly fond of. They suggested that I spend my time reading about IT or working on my school assignments. Since my schoolwork was nearly finished, I decided to look into IT-related topics, starting with networks, servers, and Wi-Fi. That is actually how I first encountered programming,while reading about networks, server types, and Wi-Fi. I also searched for information on YouTube and discovered videos that really caught my interest. I began watching content about programming in general — exploring the concepts behind it, how it works, and why it is so intriguing. I found it truly fascinating, and at that point, I decided to learn how to program.

My first assistant was Copilot. Together, we learned a lot of stuff about programming and created a rather interesting project. I spent days writing code alongside Copilot, and the final result was quite good. Looking back now, however, I consider that project to have been terrible, as I wasted far too much time fixing every single error in the code. Even so, I learned a lot from it; the experience prompted me to reflect more deeply on the nature of programming and how I could improve my own skills. I also realized that AI is not as efficient as many people believe; it makes a lot of mistakes. That is why I decided to learn how to program properly and grow as a programmer. AI is useful, but it should always be viewed as an assistant rather than a magical solution. These were some of the conclusions I reached by the end of my internship at OOZ.

Furthermore, my teachers recommended that I enroll in the MBO4 level rather than MBO3. At the time, I was very uncertain about which level to choose, primarily because I was concerned about my Dutch language proficiency. However, after visiting a class and asking the questions I needed to, I decided to go for it. Following the summer break, I dove straight into programming. It was difficult to work on my own, but I refused to give up. I began reviewing all the fundamental concepts from scratch, and my drive to truly understand them led me to want to take on another project.



My goals and ideas


Here, I would like to dive deeper into my current work, projects, concepts, and ideas, as well as how I bring each of them to life. I’ll start with my first project from the MBO4 Software Developer course: DinoInfoDex. I’ll admit, the name sounds quite similar to sites like "Pokédex" and the underlying concept is also quite alike—though there are many key differences. For instance, my project allows users to toggle between light, dark, and system-default display modes, a feature that is not present in "Pokédex". Furthermore, in DinoInfoDex, I’ve incorporated elements like a community section, an FAQ, and the ability for users to contribute to the information provided for each dinosaur species; this, in fact, explains the project's name.

The project also supports four languages: Spanish, English, Portuguese, and French (which was the latest update I implemented). All the information featured in the project is sourced from Wikipedia—a reliable, publicly accessible resource. This streamlines my workflow, as I generally don’t need to seek permission from individual authors, which would otherwise be a very time-consuming process. To be honest, this project was far from easy; I spent two weeks coding it (with the help of Copilot and YouTube tutorials). When I finally tested it, I wasn't happy with the result at all, so I scrapped everything and spent another week redesigning it to match the vision I truly wanted. I watched numerous tutorials and absorbed as much as I could. I then integrated code snippets from these tutorials while adding my own unique touches—such as the project’s theme, color palette, and information, etc.

However, some things weren’t working quite right. I began to explore VS Code, the software I use for my projects (including this website). Then I discovered something interesting: it contained all the necessary information regarding "agents" (AI-powered coding assistants). I started using them for my project. I must admit, the process wasn’t always as clean as I would have liked. Some tasks were quite complex to handle on my own — such as translating the information for each of the 200 species on the site into four different languages, or connecting the website to a data source to retrieve that information. Consequently, I spent another three weeks getting the site to function exactly as I had envisioned. Although my understanding of programming was still quite abstract, I had built the foundation myself, with the help of tutorials. While learning from tutorials isn’t always the ideal way to master programming, it’s certainly better than nothing.

About a month ago, I started learning to program on my phone using an app called Mimo. Yeah, I know ,that name might sound a bit funny, I gradually began to grasp concepts like if, else, const, let, and console.log. This gave me more confidence in my work, as I was no longer just fumbling in the dark. When I finished the project, I presented it to my teacher and explained how I had built it. He then asked me about topics like GDPR, licensing, copyright, GitHub, and various other tools—things I hadn’t even realized were part of programming. I began targeting additional tasks to better understand how to approach future projects, eventually moving on to learn programming through Codecademy. Since then, I’ve continued to build my foundational knowledge — using both Mimo and Codecademy. I now feel much more confident working with code, and I have a solid grasp of many more concepts and how to apply them.

I want to improve DinoInfoDex for many reasons, but the most significant ones include flaws in the translations into other languages, as well as issues with searching for species and using categories (such as country, length, weight, or the period in which a dinosaur lived). What bothers me most, however, is that the site takes too long to load. For this reason, I want to enhance my skills in JavaScript, CSS, and HTML by working on an increasing number of projects and exercises, watching tutorials, and seeking advice from my peers, more experienced programmers, and my teachers.
